# ChatGPT Wallet limited rollout through merchant checkout

Developed with American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa, ChatGPT Wallet begins a limited rollout as OpenAI works to make retailers' existing systems compatible with AI agents.

OpenAI is giving its artificial intelligence (AI) shopping agents a way to pay through retailers’ checkout systems, marking another shift in the ChatGPT maker’s evolving agentic commerce strategy.

Launched Nov. 13 in partnership with American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa, ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI’s “always-on” personal agents, known as dots, complete purchases on shoppers’ behalf.

Rather than keeping transactions inside ChatGPT, the virtual wallet works through merchants’ existing checkouts. Businesses retain their customer relationships, along with “control over pricing, fulfillment, returns, and customer service,” OpenAI said in its announcement.

For now, Wallet is rolling out to a limited number of U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers. While shoppers can add a payment card for agents to use, they must still approve each purchase before a transaction can be completed. The company has yet to identify any specific retailers accepting ChatGPT Wallet transactions.

Alongside the launch, OpenAI also released a Commerce Partner Playbook aimed at helping businesses prepare for a future of more agent-led purchases.

## How ChatGPT Wallet works

The launch follows a change in OpenAI’s checkout plans earlier this year. After introducing Instant Checkout in September 2025, the company shifted toward transactions through merchant apps in March.

ChatGPT Wallet builds on that approach. “We’re building agent-led commerce to fit reliably into existing systems,” OpenAI said in its announcement.

According to the company, roughly 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, including consumers researching and comparing products. Wallet aims to take that shopping activity a step further, allowing agents to complete purchases, it said.

Before checkout, shoppers review the items, total and payment card. They then authorize the purchase through a security check, such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan.

Once approved, the payment network generates a tokenized credential in place of the customer’s actual card details. Neither the merchant nor the AI agent receives those credentials, OpenAI said.

“Users keep their card’s existing rewards and protections for eligible disputes with businesses, for example when an item is defective,” the company added. Banks and payment networks, meanwhile, continue evaluating transactions.

By starting with a limited rollout, OpenAI said it hopes to “learn from real-world use” and improve compatibility with businesses and payment providers.

Eventually, the company expects users to be able to delegate purchases entirely to their agents, with limits on spending, product categories or time periods. OpenAI said those transactions would still require “enhanced safeguards,” including biometric checks and records of authorization.

## What ChatGPT Wallet means for online retailers

Keeping checkout in retailers’ hands may address one concern about agentic commerce. But that doesn’t mean every business is ready — or willing — to accept purchases made by AI agents.

Amazon, for example, blocked Meta’s Muse AI agent from shopping on its website in September, citing unauthorized activity and privacy and security concerns.

Other retailers may block legitimate transactions unintentionally. OpenAI acknowledged that existing security and fraud prevention systems can interfere with agent-led purchases.

Its new Commerce Partner Playbook offers guidance on making products accessible to agents and supporting payments through existing systems.

For starters, retailers can claim and verify their businesses in ChatGPT, then provide current product information, including pricing and availability.

But making products discoverable doesn’t guarantee agents can access a website. Retailers may need to configure their content delivery networks (CDNs) or firewalls to recognize signed requests through Web Bot Auth, a method for verifying AI agent identities. According to OpenAI, browser access doesn’t require a plugin or product feed.

Payment acceptance presents another hurdle. Fraud detection tools may flag legitimate agent purchases, leading to declined or canceled transactions.

To reduce those risks, OpenAI said it is working with payment providers Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe, along with fraud prevention companies Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd.

Merchants can also add WebMCP tools or plugins to “let agents check availability, manage carts, or take other actions” through existing systems, the company said.

## Payment companies expand their agentic commerce efforts

ChatGPT Wallet arrives as payment networks develop their own tools for AI-initiated transactions.

In April, Visa introduced Intelligent Commerce Connect, offering businesses a single integration for agent-led transactions. It followed in June with a partnership to bring Visa’s payment capabilities into OpenAI experiences.

Mastercard, meanwhile, introduced Agent Connect in September to connect merchants with AI agents, digital platforms and payment providers.

The push extends beyond consumer shopping. In October, Visa, BILL and Billtrust launched a live pilot enabling authorized AI agents to complete approved invoice payments through supplier portals.
