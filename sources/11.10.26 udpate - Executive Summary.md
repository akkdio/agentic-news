### Executive Summary

The overarching message is that **agentic commerce has crossed from concept to reality, but volumes remain tiny today**. Nearly every major player across AI, networks, issuers, merchants, PSPs, and fintechs is investing heavily because they believe AI agents will become a major commerce channel. The near-term focus is less about transaction volume and more about **trust, standards, identity, authorization, and ecosystem integration**.

For someone focused on fraud, risk, and payments, the most important takeaway is that the industry is rapidly converging on a model where **trusted, permissioned agents transact on behalf of consumers**, with payment networks, wallets, and PSPs positioning themselves as the trust and authentication layer.

------

## Key Themes Across the Ecosystem

### 1. Agentic commerce is real, but still very early

Most companies reported strong interest but negligible transaction volume.

Examples:

- Uber fulfilled only **~500 AI-generated Uber Eats orders** and **4 ride transactions** in a major week.
- Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Shopify, Amazon, and Affirm all described current volumes as small.
- The consensus view is that adoption could accelerate rapidly once trust, standards, and merchant readiness improve.

**Bottom line:** Everyone sees a large future opportunity, but meaningful GMV impact is likely years, not months, away.

------

### 2. Meta/Muse is emerging as the early breakout player

Meta's Muse generated the strongest adoption metrics:

- 10 million users across US and Canada.
- \#1 App Store position since launch.
- Roughly 25% daily active usage.
- Shopping is the most common use case.
- More than 25% of users shop through Muse.
- Average basket size exceeds $200.

Meta also noted:

- Plaid is seeing unprecedented connector activity.
- Stripe had to increase transaction limits due to higher-than-expected volumes.

**Implication:** Muse is becoming the primary proof point that consumers will allow AI agents to shop for them.

------

### 3. Trust is the industry's biggest challenge

Virtually every company identified trust as the gating factor.

Common questions:

- Is this a legitimate agent?
- What exactly was the customer's intent?
- Is the agent acting within authorized limits?
- Who is liable when the agent makes a mistake?

Visa, Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Adyen, Bank of America, Chime, and Affirm all highlighted these concerns.

The emerging industry model includes:

| Component            | Purpose                          |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Identity             | Verify customer and agent        |
| Authorization        | Define what the agent may do     |
| Intent verification  | Capture what the customer wanted |
| Tokenization         | Secure payment credentials       |
| Traceability         | Track agent involvement          |
| Liability frameworks | Resolve disputes and mistakes    |

------

### 4. Networks are positioning themselves as the trust layer

#### Visa

Visa appears furthest along operationally:

- ~50 AI partners already in production.
- Visa Intelligent Commerce platform.
- Trusted Agent Protocol.
- Agent Directory.
- Agent-specific tokens and credentials.
- B2B pilots with Ramp, BILL, Airwallex, and Billtrust.

Visa's message:

> Agentic commerce only scales if Visa provides trust across the user, agent, and transaction.

#### Mastercard

Mastercard is taking a similar path with:

- Agent Pay
- Agent Connect
- Verifiable Intent
- Agentic Trust Intelligence

A major theme in Mastercard's commentary was opposition to intermediary virtual card approaches, preferring fully tokenized network-native solutions.

------

### 5. Intent verification is becoming the new authentication

A recurring theme was that traditional cardholder authentication is insufficient when an autonomous agent acts for the customer.

Examples:

- Visa: Intent capture + biometric signatures.
- Mastercard: Verifiable Intent.
- Amex: Intent Intelligence Framework.
- PayPal: Trust layer between consumer agents and merchant agents.

**Important fraud implication:** Future risk models may rely as much on validating intent as validating identity.

The key question becomes:

> "Did the agent purchase exactly what the customer authorized?"

rather than:

> "Did the cardholder authenticate?"

------

### 7. Payments companies see agentic commerce as an opportunity, not a threat

#### PayPal

PayPal's message was straightforward:

- Integrating with major AI agents.
- Trust and security become more important when software is transacting.
- Extending beyond commerce into peer-to-peer and money movement.

#### Amex

Focus areas:

- Embedding Amex membership benefits inside AI experiences.
- OpenAI partnership expansion.

- Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE).

They are positioning themselves as:

> "The trusted payment method inside AI ecosystems."

------

### 8. Merchant discoverability may become more important than checkout

Several players noted that payment mechanics are becoming easier than product discovery.

Mastercard specifically said:

- Merchant discoverability lags payment enablement.

Shopify said:

- AI channels send consumers directly to product pages.
- Conversion rates are 64% higher from AI-referred shoppers.
- 74% of AI-driven purchases came from outside their top 100 categories.

**Important commerce insight:** Agentic commerce may dramatically benefit long-tail merchants because agents can surface niche products much more effectively than traditional search.

------

### 9. Fraud and bot management become more complex

Adyen summarized one of the most important challenges:

> Historically bots were bad. Now some bots are legitimate buyers.

This creates a new fraud problem:

- Distinguishing authorized shopping agents from malicious automation.
- Identifying agent identity.
- Preventing agent impersonation.
- Managing agent velocity and abuse.
- Detecting unauthorized delegated purchases.

For companies like PayPal, PSPs, and fraud vendors, this may create an entirely new category of risk controls.

------

### 10. BNPL providers see AI as a distribution engine

Affirm's view was notable:

- Agents will increasingly select financing options.
- Agents may optimize for:
  - APR
  - Monthly payment
  - Loan term
  - Consumer preferences

Affirm is building APIs that expose financing offers to agents and believes transparent products benefit when agents compare options objectively.

## What Matters Most for Fraud & Payments (PayPal Lens)

The most relevant implications for PayPal and fraud organizations are:

### Emerging control layers

Future agentic transactions will likely require:

1. Agent identity verification
2. Delegated authority management
3. Intent verification
4. Transaction-level permissioning
5. Merchant-agent authentication
6. Agent-specific liability frameworks
7. Agent behavioral risk scoring

### New fraud signals

Potential future signals include:

- Agent registration status
- Prior agent behavior
- User-to-agent relationship history
- Scope of delegated permissions
- Intent matching confidence
- Agent reputation scores
- Cross-network agent activity

### Strategic takeaway

The industry appears to be converging on a future where:

> Payments are no longer just consumer → merchant transactions.

Instead they become:

> **Consumer → trusted agent → merchant agent → merchant**

The companies that own the trust, identity, authorization, and intent layers (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Stripe, and major wallet providers) are positioning themselves to become the core infrastructure of this new commerce model.

For PayPal specifically, this update reinforces that **agent identity, delegated authority, and intent verification may become as important as traditional fraud controls such as device fingerprinting, account takeover detection, and payment risk models.**

The card networks are trying to become the trust and identity layer.

```

   ↓

   ↓

   ↓
```

- Agent identity registries
- Verifiable intent
- Dispute frameworks
- Global acceptance

From Visa's perspective:

### Meta Position

Meta is taking the opposite approach.

Their implicit position:

```
Consumer
   ↓
Muse
   ↓
Payment Rail
```

Meta wants Muse to become the primary consumer interface.

The phrase that jumped out:

> Monetization will come from transaction fees paid by businesses.

That's Amazon-like thinking.

Meta ultimately wants merchants paying for access to demand.

### Advantage

**Near term:** Meta.

Consumers choose agents before they choose payment rails.

**Long term:** Visa/Mastercard.

History suggests interfaces change, but payment acceptance infrastructure endures.

# Most Important Payments Debate:

# Network Tokens vs Virtual Cards

This is probably the biggest technical debate hidden in the update.

------

## Mastercard is clearly attacking Stripe's model

You can almost read between the lines.

Mastercard repeatedly references:

> one-time virtual cards are a temporary bridge

and

> we do not support intermediary VCNs as a long-term solution

That is directed squarely at approaches similar to Stripe.

------

# How Stripe's Virtual Card Model Works

Simplified:

Customer card:

```
Amex 1234
```

Agent purchase:

```
Customer card
      ↓
Stripe
      ↓
Creates One-Time Card
      ↓
Merchant
```

Merchant never sees original credential.

Merchant receives:

```
Virtual Card
```

instead.

### Benefits

Very easy.

Works immediately.

No merchant upgrades required.

No network-level ecosystem changes.

Can onboard thousands of merchants quickly.

Good bootstrap strategy.

------

## Downsides

### 1. Payment credential gets abstracted

Network loses visibility.

Instead of:

```
Customer → Merchant
```

you get

```
Customer → Stripe VCN → Merchant
```

Visa and Mastercard hate this.

------

### 2. Rewards optimization problems

Consumer may have:

- Amex Platinum
- Chase Sapphire
- PayPal Cashback

When an intermediary VCN is inserted:

- card controls can break
- rewards may not flow cleanly
- issuer visibility may decline

Mastercard explicitly called this out.

------

### 3. Weak agent identity

The merchant sees:

```
Virtual Card
```

but may not know:

- Which agent acted?
- What permissions existed?
- What intent was captured?

The payment instrument isn't carrying agent metadata.

------

### 4. Margin compression for networks

If Stripe owns:

- orchestration
- token issuance
- agent integration

Stripe captures more of the value chain.

Networks become commodity settlement rails.

Visa and Mastercard clearly do not want this future.

------

# Token-Based Model

Visa/Mastercard model:

```
Consumer Card
      ↓
Network Token
      ↓
Agent Metadata
      ↓
Intent Metadata
      ↓
Merchant
```

The token carries information.

Potentially:

- customer identity
- approved agent
- permissions
- intent
- authentication state

all attached to the transaction.

------

## Advantages

### Better fraud decisions

Merchant and issuer can see:

- authenticated agent
- intent signal
- token provenance

Much richer risk signals.

For someone in fraud, this is huge.

------

### Better dispute handling

Future dispute:

> Agent ordered wrong item.

Token model can answer:

- Which agent?
- What instructions?
- Who approved?

VCN model is weaker.

------

### Better network economics

Keeps Visa/Mastercard central.

Not surprisingly their preferred outcome.

------

## Downside

Massive ecosystem coordination.

Requires:

- Issuers
- Acquirers
- PSPs
- Merchants
- Agents

all upgrading.

This takes years.

------

# My Prediction

### 2026-2028

Stripe-like virtual card / delegated card approaches dominate.

Why?

They're deployable today.

No ecosystem change needed.

Merchants already accept cards.

------

### 2028-2032

Network tokenization likely wins for scale.

Why?

The fraud, liability, and intent problems become too important.

As agent volumes grow, networks will force standardization around:

- Agent identity
- Intent verification
- Delegated authority
- Tokenized credentials

# The Fraud Angle (Most Relevant to PayPal)

The most important observation is that **tokens are not really about payments. They're about identity and intent.**

Traditional fraud stacks answer:

> Is this customer legitimate?

Agentic commerce requires answering:

> Is this customer legitimate?
>
> Is this agent legitimate?
>
> Was this agent authorized?
>
> Did this action match intent?
>
> If not, who is liable?

Network-token approaches are being designed around these questions.

Virtual-card approaches are mainly solving:

> How do we get transactions flowing quickly?

That's why Stripe's model may dominate adoption initially, while Visa/Mastercard's token model may ultimately dominate risk management and ecosystem scaling. For companies like PayPal, Adyen, Riskified, and other fraud providers, the real long-term opportunity is likely in the **agent identity + intent verification layer**, not the payment credential itself.
