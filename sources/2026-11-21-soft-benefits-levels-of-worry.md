# Soft benefits for agents + Levels of Worry

## How do we make the "soft" benefits legible to AI agents?

How do we compress the soft benefits different providers offer into something AI agents can understand and use in comparisons? If we don't figure this out, only the biggest companies will win, because they win on what AI agents already easily understand: Price, availability, and speed of fulfillment.

Shopify has to be working on this. It's the only way its merchants beat Amazon and Walmart in agentic commerce. You can see early versions in its Universal Commerce Protocol (co-developed with Google) and in tools like Knowledge Base that let merchants control what agents say about their brand.

Financial services will need its own equivalent, or everyone except Chase will lose.

## The Levels of Worry

Maybe the way to think about personal AI assistants is by the levels of worry they can take off your shoulders:

**Level One:** "Did it understand me, and can it do the thing?" The fix is a capable AI agent with a high success rate.

**Level Two:** "What happens if it acts and screws up?" The fix is near-perfect performance (unlikely) or robust liability protection (more likely).

**Level Three:** "I'm worried about something and don't have a solution." The fix is an AI agent that helps you come up with the solution and carries it out.

**Level Four:** "Catch it before I even know to worry." The fix is a proactive AI agent with enough context on your life, enough trust that you delegate to it, and the ability to act reliably.

Navan has a cool (narrow) example of level four. If your flight is delayed and you'll get to the hotel after midnight, its AI voice agent calls the hotel to hold the room so the reservation isn't canceled. Then it tells you, and lets you listen to a recording of the call, just to cement that trust. Neat! Imagine if every possible problem in your life got intercepted that way, before it turned into a worry.
