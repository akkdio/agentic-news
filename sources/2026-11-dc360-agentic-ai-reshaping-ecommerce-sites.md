EDITOR’S NOTE
One year ago, the online retail industry entered the 2025 holiday 
season at the close of a breakout year for artificial intelligence 
(AI)-driven referral traffic. Salesforce estimated that AI and 
agents assisted with 17% of all orders in the U.S. during that 
year’s Cyber Week, which it defines as the Tuesday before 
Thanksgiving and the six days that follow. That share was worth 
roughly $13.5 billion in sales.
All of that ecommerce activity occurred in the wake of ballooning 
AI traffic volumes to retailers’ websites, which Adobe Analytics 
reported to be up by four-digit percentages year over year during 
previous periods in 2025 from 2024 levels.
In 2026, Adobe predicted that holiday season referrals from 
AI sources would be up by 160% from a year ago. However, 
conversion metrics are attracting an increasing amount of 
attention, with Adobe pre-holiday analysis showing that AI-
referred shoppers appeared to spend more than their non-AI-
referred counterparts.
That development could characterize progress for AI agents in 
providing advanced levels of utility from a year ago. Salesforce, 
for instance, has assessed that agentic commerce can be judged 
by how well and if it operates at specific tiers, accomplishing 
basic research tasks at the lowest level and communicating with

In 2025, ecommerce sites confronted a wave of new referral traffic 
from artificial intelligence (AI) sources. During the first of that 
year, Adobe Analytics observed a 4,700% increase year over year 
in AI-driven visits to the merchants’ websites that it tracks. Flash 
forward to the month of September in 2026, and that year-over-
year increase compressed to 71% — but another important metric 
continued to grow: conversion.
Adobe noted in an October report that September’s AI-referred 
visits to ecommerce sites resulted in purchases 90% more 
often than referrals from non-AI sources. That was the highest 
advantage that Adobe’s analysts documented since they began 
measuring.
The results suggest that AI platforms, such as ChatGPT , Claude 
and Gemini are getting better at recommending products that 
online shoppers want. Meanwhile, retailers see an opportunity 
to meet that traffic, which in some cases may be coming from AI 
agents that are pursuing products on behalf of a human shopper 
who may not have even physically clicked on a link or looked at 
the merchant’s website on a web browser.

WALMART’S AGENTIC-SPECIFIC  
SETTINGS AND RULES
Agentic commerce, where human shoppers give AI agents 
permission to research and purchase on their behalf, has sparked 
two different responses from North America’s two largest online 
retailers. Amazon, which has blocked third-party agents from 
sources such as OpenAI and Meta, prefers to push its shoppers 
toward Amazon-controlled AI assistants. In contrast, Walmart, 
which has its own AI assistants, has embraced these agents, in 
some cases working directly with technology providers to supply 
third-party agents with data and access to its ecosystem.
In October, Walmart took a major step forward with this strategy, 
announcing new tools for its shoppers to use to manage 
permissions and settings for their agents. The launches included 
what Walmart called Agent Profiles and an Agent Gateway, along 
with rules for how agents would be allowed to access certain data.
“We’ve always aimed to be where our customers are, from choosing 
where to build stores and clubs, to launching e-commerce when 
customers went online, to services like Muse, ChatGPT , and Gemini 
as conversational commerce emerged, ” said Daniel Danker, 
executive vice president of AI acceleration, product and design at 
Walmart. “Today, we’re building on this by making it possible for 
customers to shop using AI agents, while keeping customers in 
control. ”

Agent Profiles, for instance, will be created whenever a new agent 
representing a customer first visits Walmart’s ecommerce site. 
Customers then see the agent’s profile through their Walmart 
account and are given the options to toggle spending limits, view 
purchase histories or delete a profile altogether.
Meanwhile, the Agent Gateway is designed to give agents faster 
paths to accomplishing their tasks, leveraging Model Context 
Protocol (MCP), which is an open standard developed by Anthropic. 
The protocol allows AI applications to connect with external 
tools and services for things like discovery, adding items to carts, 
purchasing and order tracking.
NEWEGG OPENS UP TO AGENTIC PAYMENTS
The consumer electronics seller Newegg sees Google’s AI 
recommendations from Search, AI Mode and Gemini as places 
where it wants to enable purchases, so in October the company 
announced that it would integrate Google’s Universal Commerce 
Protocol (UCP).
The decision placed Newegg alongside prominent retailers, such 
as Wayfair, Walmart, Macy’s and more, that have supported UCP 
since Google formally launched it in January. Google initially co-
developed UCP with teams from Walmart and Wayfair, as well as 
Etsy, Shopify and Target.
The UCP integration means that Newegg can ensure it remains the 
merchant of record, while providing Google with product catalog 
data to inform the search giant’s AI-powered shopping experiences.

HOW ONLINE SHOPPERS 
SAY THEY ARE USING AI
Artificial intelligence (AI) appears on ecommerce sites through 
features that may be obvious, such as chatbot assistants, or 
invisibly through backend features and fulfillment operations. 
However, as 2026 draws to a close, online shoppers shared what 
they see and what they value when it comes to making purchases.
New survey results from Digital Commerce 360 and Bizrate Insights 
reflect answers from 1,096 online shoppers who participated in an 
October study addressing the state of AI on the ecommerce sites 
that shoppers use.
The results show that when it comes to improving ecommerce 
experiences, relevance and efficiency outweigh other factors when 
shoppers decide how and where to buy products online. They also 
highlight what online retailers should prioritize when building out 
their sites with new AI-powered tools.
MEETING SHOPPERS IN MOBILE CONTEXTS
First, online shoppers made clear in their responses that mobile 
devices are where the bulk of their digital shopping activity gets 
done. Asked which type of device they used most often when they 
shop online, 58% said smartphones. 37% said desktops or laptops, 
and another 5% cited tablets.

One of the most high-profile AI agents launched in 2027 came 
from Meta, the Facebook parent company that called its latest 
product Muse. The company leveraged its existing family of apps 
to encourage users to try Muse, giving it access to data, credentials 
and payment info to manage tasks. By mid-October, Meta claimed 
that Muse had amassed 10 million users, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg 
asserting that 25% of that base were using the agent daily.
Though it is not clear what share of those users are letting Muse 
execute transactions or successfully buying items from holiday gift 
lists, the initial numbers still show interest exists and Muse has 
early reach.
As Meta’s online advertising competitor, Google, continues to push 
features into its AI products and other AI companies to do the same, 
the coming holiday season and year that follows will test just how 
much value online shoppers see from these agents.
Nevertheless, online shoppers who do wish to end agents out onto 
the internet to fill their carts and check out for them now numerous 
options from names they already rely on for daily work and social 
media.
If Adobe’s reading of its conversion data remains sound, agents 
may already be building that trust. Still, making purchases is only 
one milestone for online shopping journeys. Particularly in January,
